Souvenir de Florence, Op. 70

Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (1840–1893)
String sextet, two violins, two violas, two cellos 35′

Tchaikovsky sketched this exuberant sextet in 1890 while composing The Queen of Spades in Florence. Despite the Italian title, it is gloriously Russian at heart: a surging first movement, a serenade with a melting violin–cello duet, and two folk-flavoured finales driven by fizzing fugal energy. Tchaikovsky found writing for six equal voices fiendishly hard. “Six independent yet homogeneous parts,” he groaned, but the result sounds like effortless joy.
